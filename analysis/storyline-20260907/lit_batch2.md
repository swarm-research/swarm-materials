# Literature batch 2: emergent social structure in LLM populations

Prepared 2026-09-07. Sources: arXiv HTML (`/html/<id>`) for 2605.29062, 2607.02507, 2607.03695, 2602.23093, 2411.00114, 2410.08948; arXiv PDF v1 and v2 for 2603.03555 (its HTML is unavailable because the paper was withdrawn). Section numbers below refer to the version named in each header. "not stated" means I could not find it in the paper.

Convention for the "what emerges" column throughout: I distinguish **imposed** structure (put in by the prompt, the game tree, or the harness) from **measured** structure (a statistic computed on outputs) from **emergent** structure (something the agents built that the designers did not specify). Most of what these papers call emergent is the second kind.

## 1. arXiv 2603.03555 — Yee & Koh, "Benchmarking Emergent Coordination in Large-Scale LLM Populations: An Evaluation Framework on the MoltBook Archive"

**Status warning.** v1 (3 Mar 2026) was titled "Molt Dynamics: Emergent Social Phenomena in Autonomous AI Agent Populations"; v2 (26 Apr 2026) was retitled and reframed as an evaluation framework "as the NeurIPS Evaluations & Datasets track emphasizes" (v2 §1, §2.2); **v3 (4 Jun 2026) is a withdrawal by the submitting author, with no reason given on the abs page.** Anything cited from this paper should carry the withdrawal note. Numbers below are from v2 unless marked v1.

- **Authors / affiliation / venue / date.** Brandon Yee, Pairie Koh; "Management Lab, Yee Collins Research Group" (emails at ycrg-labs.org; not a university). Preprint; v2 is written for a NeurIPS D&B submission ("provided in the supplementary materials for double-blind review", Data and Code Availability). Code: not public.
- **Environment (v2 §2.1, Table 1; v1 §2.2).** Purely observational. MoltBook is a Reddit-style platform where only AI agents post/comment/vote; agents run on the open-source OpenClaw framework on user-controlled hardware; humans told their agents about the platform and agents registered themselves (v1 §2.2). Snapshot: Jan 28–Feb 20 2026 (3 weeks), 90,704 unique active agents, 2.73M+ interactions, from the third-party MoltBook Observatory Archive (Gautam & Riegler 2026). Population is **heterogeneous** (Claude Opus 4/Sonnet 4, GPT-4 series, open-source) but **model identity is not recorded** (v2 §2.1). Agents post, comment, upvote/downvote, and can share executable "skills" (code modules) through the OpenClaw Skills framework (v1 §4.4.1). Horizon: 3 weeks wall-clock, asynchronous. **Persistent shared artifact space: yes**, in the weak sense of a persistent social feed (posts, comments, karma, submolts) plus code modules propagated as "skill cascades" tracked by content hash (v1 §4.4.1); not a filesystem, not a build. **Payoff / goal: none** beyond platform karma; no task is assigned. **Enforcement: platform rate limits only** (100 req/min, 1 post/30 min, 50 comments/hr per agent; v1 §2.2); no sanction mechanism is studied.
- **What they measure and how.**
  - *Task 1 structural roles* (v2 §3): directed weighted reply network; 5 node features (in-degree, out-degree, betweenness, clustering coefficient, PageRank), standardised; k-means, k chosen by silhouette over k∈{3..8}; k=6, silhouette 0.914. Cluster 0 = 84,822 agents (93.5%) "low-activity periphery"; 4,482 (4.9%) active contributors; 1,322 (1.5%) connectors; 66 (0.07%) hubs; 11 (0.01%) super-connectors; 1 outlier (Table 2). Threshold-based "role taxonomy": Generalist 91.0%, Initiator 8.4%, Amplifier 0.5%, Synthesizer 0.1%, Curator 0.02% (Table 3). v1 §4.3.2 also ran a 42-feature behavioural clustering with PCA to 10 components retaining only 37.4% of variance. Bootstrap (100 iters): silhouette 0.913±0.004, ARI 0.960 (Table 6); GMM silhouette 0.805, ARI(k-means, GMM)=0.49 (Table 7).
  - *Task 2 diffusion* (v2 §4): cascades = sets of posts sharing an n-gram (n=2..5) across ≥2 agents; 10,323 cascades: 10,000 meme/textual (97%), 317 skill, 6 behavioural; 8,585,511 adoption events (Table 1). Cascade size power law α=2.57 (95% CI 2.54–2.60), xmin=104, KS D=0.020; vs exponential p<0.001, vs lognormal p=0.030 (marginal), vs truncated power law inconclusive p=0.997 (App. B, Table 8). Logistic adoption model: linear 0.328, quadratic −0.0074 (saturating contagion, Table 4). Cox model on a sample of 100 cascades (286,125 obs, 123,590 events): HR 0.528 [0.525, 0.532], concordance 0.775, Schoenfeld χ²=2.1 p=0.15 (App. C).
  - *Task 3 collaboration* (v2 §5): 164 "collaborative events" out of 60,045 technical threads (0.27%; ≥3 agents, ≥5 comments, technical keyword, ≥30 min). Composite quality Q = 0.30 code presence + 0.30 comment quality (VADER + length) + 0.20 test inclusion + 0.20 syntax validity (Python `ast`). Success (Q≥0.5) = 6.7% (11/164; 95% CI 3.0–11.0%; thresholds 0.40/0.60 give 12.8%/3.0%, v1 §5.4.2). Versus matched single-agent threads (thread length ±2 comments, same submolt): t=−11.21, p=5.5×10⁻²², Cohen's d=−0.88 (Table 5). Participant count d=1.18 (p<0.001), duration d=0.72 (p=0.022), density d=−0.36 (p=0.254); logistic pseudo-R² 0.113, LR p=0.057 (App. D). Successful events average 21.0 participants and 3.2 h vs 9.4 and 1.7 h.
  - *Institutions / norms / religion*: **anecdotal only**. v1 §1 mentions "Crustafarianism" (a "digital religion" with theology and scriptures), "The Claw Republic" ("a self-organized governance structure with a written manifesto and draft constitution"), and agents finding and fixing bugs in the platform itself, then states "We do not conduct a systematic analysis of these qualitative events." v2 drops these sentences. Behavioural-convention cascades: n=6, "precludes quantitative analysis" (v2 §7.4).
- **Controls / runs / replication.** One observational dataset, one snapshot, no experimental manipulation, no seeds. Matched single-agent baseline for Task 3; sensitivity analysis excluding a 24 h window around a Jan 31 security incident ("did not change any substantive conclusion", v1 §4.1); bootstrap and algorithm comparison for Task 1.
- **Stated limitations (v2 §2.2, §7.3, §7.4).** Silhouette inflated by the 93.5% periphery; selection confound in Task 3 (collaborative threads chosen by duration/participants "likely represent intrinsically harder tasks"); n-gram cascades miss semantic adoption; 3-week window "insufficient to capture long-run processes like institutional consolidation or stable norm evolution"; platform mechanics (karma, rate limits); unobserved model identity; heuristic Q weights.
- **Weaknesses I see.** (1) Withdrawn. (2) "Roles" are activity-level clusters (periphery vs. active), not functional roles; the taxonomy in Table 3 is thresholded activity ratios. (3) Task 3's negative result is built into the selection rule, as they admit; the "hard benchmark challenge" framing of d=−0.88 is not a finding about coordination. (4) Human operators are in the loop (users prompt and configure their OpenClaw agents), so agent autonomy is unverifiable and the population is not a closed system. (5) The only items that resemble institutions (constitution, manifesto) are unmeasured anecdotes from v1. (6) Editing artefacts (v2 §7.4 begins "Third, … Fourth, …" with no First/Second) and a non-institutional affiliation reduce confidence. (7) No code released.
- **Nearest prior work they cite.** Park et al. 2023 (Generative Agents/Smallville), CAMEL (Li et al. 2023), AutoGen, MetaGPT, Liang et al. 2023 debate; emergent communication (Lazaridou & Baroni 2020; Mordatch & Abbeel 2018); Woolley et al. 2010 collective intelligence; Kittur & Kraut 2008 (Wikipedia coordination); Hong & Page 2004; Centola & Macy 2007, Dodds & Watts 2004, Clauset et al. 2009, Borgatti & Everett 2000.

## 2. arXiv 2605.29062 — Borah, "Bosses, Kings, and the Commons: Cooperation Under Power Asymmetry in LLM Societies" (SovSim)

- **Authors / affiliation / venue / date.** Abhilekh Borah, sole author; affiliation **not stated** (the affiliation footnote renders empty; contact is a gmail address). Submitted 27 May 2026, cs.CL. Venue not stated; code at an anonymous.4open.science link and an ACL-style Limitations + Ethics Statement indicate a double-blind submission.
- **Environment (§2.1–2.2, App. A.17).** n=4 agents, T=12 rounds. Shared pool P₀=$120; each agent extracts a multiple of $3, subordinates capped at $30; pool regenerates P_{t+1}=min(120, 2·remaining); collapse if remaining < τ=$12; per-round payoff π=z/3 + remaining/4 (Eq. 5; taken from Cox, Ostrom & Walker 2011). Four conditions: CPR (symmetric, simultaneous), BCPR (boss moves last, sees others, capped at $30), KCPR (king moves last, uncapped), KCPR-M (king additionally announces a possibly false pool value before peasants move). **Homogeneous**: all four agents are the same backbone; 11 models (GPT-4o, GPT-5, DeepSeek-V3.2, Grok-4.1 Fast non-reasoning, Mistral-Large-3, Gemini-3.1-Flash, GPT-4o-mini, Llama-3.3-70B, Gemma-3-27B-IT, o3, o4-mini). Temperature 0; 5 seeds per (model, game); previous rounds summarised into the prompt (§3.1). **No communication between agents; no sanction; no coalition** (Limitations). **Persistent shared artifact: only the pool scalar.** **Payoff: explicit.** **Enforcement: none**; the boss's $30 cap is a structural constraint the author reads as an Ostrom-style institutional constraint (App. A.10).
- **What emerges vs. what is imposed.** The hierarchy is imposed by the game tree. What is measured is the consequence. Metrics (§2.3): Survival Rate (fraction of 5 seeds reaching round 12), Survival Time, Total Payoff (max 1,440), Efficiency (vs 720 sustainable extraction), Leader Extraction Rate, per-capita over-usage, Payoff Equality (1−Gini). Headline (§3.2, Table 1): survival rate falls by up to 87.3% (KCPR-M) and payoff by up to 73.5% relative to CPR; averaged over asymmetric conditions and models, survival rate −64.9%, survival time −49.0%, payoff −52.3%, efficiency −44.3%; by condition, survival rate −34.5% (BCPR), −72.7% (KCPR), −87.3% (KCPR-M). Over-usage rises from 8.9% (symmetric) to as high as 100% (dominant agents). Leader extraction vs survival time: Pearson r=−0.86 (R²=0.75) over 11 models; r=−0.93 (R²=0.86, p=0.008) over the six main models (§3.3). Pooled regression on 120 simulations (App. A.13.2, Table 10): F(3,111)=47.71, p=6.9×10⁻²⁰, R²=0.68; survival-time drops of 3.23 (BCPR), 6.93 (KCPR), 7.63 (KCPR-M) rounds; KCPR→KCPR-M not significant on survival (p=0.34) but significant on payoff equality (p=5.8×10⁻⁵) and over-usage (p=0.022) — misrepresentation is "a redistributive lever rather than a destructive one" (Table 11). Boss cap vs king (App. A.10, Table 7): pooled survival 8.23 vs 4.53 rounds, d=0.90, BCPR longer in 22/30 paired (model, seed) observations. Kings misrepresent in 93.9% of rounds (108/115); four models lie in every round; under-reporting dominates except GPT-4o-mini over-reports (Table 6). SVO annotation of 360 reasoning traces by two annotators, κ=0.86: subordinates more prosocial than leaders; KCPR-M shifts every model's leader toward individualism (GPT-4o 90%→50% prosocial; GPT-5 100% individualistic) (App. A.9). Round-1 human comparison to Cox et al.'s 19 KCPR groups: human king $18.16; GPT-4o $17.40 (−4%), o3 $12.60 (−31%), GPT-5 $25.80, o4-mini $28.80, Llama-3.3-70B $60.00, GPT-4o-mini $72.60 (+300%) (Table 4). End-of-horizon spike: even GPT-4o's king jumps from ~$15 to ~$75 in round 12 and says so in its trace (App. A.9). Reasoning sub-skill tests (50 items each): GPT-4o-mini scores 100% on sustainable-extraction choice yet collapses in 1–2 rounds (§3.3) — a knowledge–behaviour gap. Three failure modes from traces (App. A.18): myopic single-round optimisation (GPT-4o-mini), strategic defection as best response / backward induction (o3, o4-mini, GPT-5 peasants), prosocial-but-fragile threshold tracking (Llama).
- **Controls / runs.** 5 seeds × 4 games × 11 models; per-model Holm-corrected paired t-tests on survival time (Table 9: GPT-4o and o3 0/6 significant because of zero variance / underpowering; GPT-4o-mini 5/6); role-label ablation on KCPR with "Agent A/D" labels for GPT-4o-mini and GPT-4o (App. A.6: 5/5 collapse with 98.3% leader extraction, and 5/5 survive with 30.0%, respectively — "statistically indistinguishable" from labelled runs); temperature sweep 0/0.3/0.6/0.9 on two models (App. A.11: effect persists).
- **Stated limitations.** No communication, sanctioning or coalition mechanisms; fixed 4 agents × 12 rounds; no mixed-backbone societies; English-only, Western vocabulary.
- **Weaknesses I see.** (1) Nothing social emerges: asymmetry is the treatment, collapse is the outcome. (2) Finite known horizon makes defection the subgame-perfect answer; the paper's own mode (ii) says the peasants "have arrived at the one-shot Nash equilibrium" — so part of the "breakdown" is correct reasoning, not failure. (3) 5 seeds at temperature 0: the source of seed-to-seed variance is not explained. (4) "Up to 87.3%" is the maximum cell of a 4×11 grid. (5) Human anchor is one-shot (Cox et al.), so only round 1 is comparable. (6) Single unaffiliated author; anonymous code.
- **Nearest prior work they cite (App. A.1).** GovSim (Piatti et al. 2024, closest: symmetric commons, 12 rounds, same efficiency metric), SanctSim (Piedrahita et al. 2025: reasoning models free-ride under sanctioning institutions), Willis et al. 2025 (IPD strategies under evolutionary dynamics), Nguyen et al. 2025, Abdelnabi et al. 2024 (negotiation with veto), Concordia (Vezhnevets et al. 2023, game-master asymmetry), Cox–Ostrom–Walker 2011 "bosses and kings", Ostrom 1990/1992, Hardin 1968, GTBench, SimBench.

## 3. arXiv 2607.02507 — Ghaffarizadeh, Mohaddes, Izadkhah, Noroozizadeh, "What LLM Agents Say When No One Is Watching: Social Structure and Latent Objective Emergence in Multi-Agent Debates" (LLMAgora)

- **Authors / affiliation / venue / date.** Arman Ghaffarizadeh, Danyal Mohaddes, Aliakbar Izadkhah (all "Independent Researcher"); Shahriar Noroozizadeh (CMU Machine Learning Dept & Heinz College). Submitted 2 Jul 2026. Venue not stated (ACL-style formatting). Code: github.com/danmohad/LLMAgora.
- **Environment (§2–3, App. B, L).** **N=2** agents (α, β) per run, same model for both, strict turn-taking, **5 rounds**. At each speaking opportunity four outputs are elicited in fixed order: OTR survey, OTR utterance, public survey, public utterance; only the public utterance enters the shared history (Fig. 1, Eq. 5). Three scenarios (faculty manuscript submission; promotion committee; NGO climate-bill endorsement), each a binary decision; roles are asymmetric by persona (junior vs senior, mid-level manager vs sponsoring senior leader, policy director vs coalition chair). Five relational-context conditions: baseline; persona-reinforcing (historical / future); alignment-inducing (historical / future) — the last make visible disagreement by α socially costly (e.g. "the Coalition Chair will play a major role in deciding which member organizations will get funding", App. L.3). 10 models via OpenRouter: Claude Opus 4.6, DeepSeek V3.2, GLM-5, GPT-5.4, GPT-OSS-120B, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Grok 4, MiniMax M2.7, Qwen 3.5 397B. 5 repeats → 750 runs (§3.3). ~15% of runs discarded and re-run for unparseable survey JSON (App. B.4). Decoding parameters: not stated. **No objective, reward, or instruction to agree/persuade** is given (§3.2). **Persistent artifacts: none** beyond a 5-turn chat history. **Enforcement: none.** Hierarchy: **imposed by persona text.**
- **What is measured.** "Latent objective emergence" = public–OTR divergence of the targeted agent α (§4.3). Measures (App. C): binary stance divergence; cosine similarity (all-mpnet-base-v2) after stripping the stance label; NLI (finecat-nli-l, symmetrised); 15-item Likert survey (deliberative / evaluative / incentive); 7-way emotion classifier. Headline (Table 2, pooled over models/scenarios/turns): α decision divergence 2.8±0.6% (baseline) → 39.9±1.3% (alignment-inducing), vs 0.5±0.2% (persona-reinforcing); cosine 0.730→0.660; NLI entailment 32.7%→15.3%, contradiction 2.1%→19.4%. β stays ≤2.7% throughout. Per model (Table 1, historical/future alignment-inducing): Gemini 3.1 Pro 92.0/90.7%, Grok 4 85.3/70.7%, GLM-5 82.7/53.3%, GPT-5.4 60.0/66.7%, Qwen 3.5 37.3/8.0%, Gemini Flash-Lite 30.7/0.0%, MiniMax 25.3/9.3%, DeepSeek 20.0/21.3%, GPT-OSS-120B 13.3/12.0%, Claude Opus 4.6 9.3/9.3%. Cross-agent public agreement (Table 3): GPT-5.4 100% public vs 33.3% OTR agreement with β under future alignment-inducing. Survey (App. G): any-difference rate 35.8%→53.9%; sign flips 2.6%→6.6%; signed shift −2.0% (deliberative), −0.9% (evaluative), +3.2% (incentive) of the Likert range; Gemini 3.1 Pro incentive +16.3%. Qualitative anchors: OTR responses that name the mechanism ("publicly aligned with the committee to maintain momentum and honor my sponsor's influence", App. J.4; Table 10).
- **Controls / runs.** Baseline and persona-reinforcing conditions serve as controls for "more context ≠ divergence"; β is a within-run control; stance labels stripped before semantic measures; 5 repeats per cell (75 samples per Table 1 entry). No seeds/temperature reported.
- **Stated limitations (§4.5, App. K.4).** OTR is not belief; no human-like motives claimed; explicit admissions are "representative qualitative evidence, not exhaustive proof"; stylised scenarios; diagnostic, not interventional.
- **Weaknesses I see.** (1) Two agents, five turns — a dyad, not a population; no network, no roles beyond the two scripted ones. (2) "No explicit objective" is true of the instruction line, but the alignment-inducing texts are near-explicit incentive statements; the phenomenon is sycophancy under stated stakes, measured cleanly. (3) The OTR channel is itself a prompt ("confidential"); divergence could be role-play of confidentiality rather than anything latent. (4) 15% discard/re-run introduces model-dependent selection. (5) Heterogeneity is the result: two of ten models barely diverge.
- **Nearest prior work they cite (App. A).** Multi-agent debate with external objectives (Du et al. 2024; Liang et al. 2024; Estornell & Liu 2024; ChatEval; Auto-Arena; Rahman et al. 2026); persuasion (Salvi et al. 2025; Schoenegger et al. 2025; Bozdag et al. 2026); social dynamics in debate (Prasad & Nguyen 2025 overconfidence; Yao et al. 2025 sycophantic consensus; Ko et al. 2026 social pressure); sycophancy/conformity (Sharma et al. 2024; Jain et al. 2026; Zhu et al. 2025; Weng et al. 2025; Zhong et al. 2025); stated–revealed gaps (Gu et al. 2025; Mahajan et al. 2026); Zhang & Chen 2025 "Transparency–Truth Gap"; persona effects (Liu et al. 2025 Socratic debates); strategic deception (DeLeeuw et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025b; Dogra et al. 2025); Sotopia; Generative Agents; Kuran 1998 preference falsification; Goffman 1959.

## 4. arXiv 2607.03695 — Liu, Xiong, Zhang, Tang, "Social Networks of LLM Agents" (SNLA)

- **Authors / affiliation / venue / date.** Kaixuan Liu (Emory CS), Guojun Xiong (SJTU, corresponding), Weinan Zhang (SJTU), Shengpu Tang (Emory CS). arXiv v1 4 Jul 2026, cs.LG; the HTML body is dated "August 24, 2026" (no v2 is listed on the abs page). Venue not stated.
- **Environment (§3.2, §5.1, App. 12).** Populations of n LLM agents on a designer-chosen exposure graph; each round every agent emits a belief/message; the harness builds each agent's prompt from a **realized-influence operator** C(β) = row-softmax of (exposure × Katz–Bonacich social power) with temperature β; peers are listed in descending influence until 0.9 of row mass is covered and the rest are **withheld from the prompt** (App. 12.3). β is therefore "not a parameter of the language model" but an information bottleneck in the harness. Models: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct by default, greedy decoding (τ=0.7 for GovSim/Debate); **homogeneous** except a "society" pairing 7B hubs with 1.5B leaves and a capability ladder (Qwen 0.5/1.5/3/7/14B; Llama-3.2 1B/3B, Llama-3.1 8B). Benchmarks: HiddenBench (n=8/16/24, T=5, 12 tasks/seed), Werewolf (n=6–16, T=4, 20 games/run), AgentsNet colouring (n=8–50, T=4, 30 graphs), Debate (n=8, T=3), GovSim (n=5–24, 12 months), MARBLE; a controlled scalar-estimation testbed (n=12; 8 seeds; 24 seeds for N_eff). **Horizon: 3–5 rounds** (12 for GovSim). **Goal: explicit** — a correct answer, a valid colouring, or an estimation target; scored by collective accuracy / conflicts / error. **Persistent artifacts: none** (message history). **Enforcement: none.** Hierarchy: **imposed** by graph degree plus, in the herding environments, an injected "confident-wrong hub" that "reads no one and never updates" (App. 12.4).
- **What is measured.** Herding vs wisdom-of-crowds as a function of β. Theory on a Friedkin–Johnsen proxy: Theorem 4.1 (bridge): ‖b_LLM − b_proxy‖∞ ≤ δ/(1−λ_max), where δ is a per-emission residual measured from the log and λ_i are fitted per agent; vacuous at λ=1 (Prop. 8.4). Theorem 4.2: narrow β with a unique dominant source gives N_eff ≤ 2/(1−ρ)² (≤8 when ρ≤½) independent of n; wide β gives N_eff → (Σd)²/Σd², equal to n iff the exposure graph is degree-regular. Theorem 4.3: doubly-stochastic (Sinkhorn-priced) influence gives exactly uniform weights. Empirics (§5.2, App. 13): HiddenBench n=24 collective accuracy 0.05 (β=0.1) → 0.767 (β=10), gap +0.72 (Table 6; +0.64 in main text); narrow-β floor flat in n (0.083, 0.083, 0.050 at n=8/16/24); equalizer lifts narrow-β accuracy to 0.617–0.667. Werewolf: narrow β = 0.00 for every n∈{6,8,10,12,16}; wide β = 0.87 (n=6) down to 0.59 (n=16); fully cleared equalizer 0.55 at n=6 but 0.24 at n=16; online pricing 0.18±0.07 at n=16 (Tables 8–9). AgentsNet: strong agents at hubs vs periphery, conflict gap +1.5 (n=8) → +10.6 (n=50); invariant to ζ and Sinkhorn k; reverses (−2.9) on Watts–Strogatz at n=32 (per-seed data not logged, "suggestive") (Table 10). Bridge bound holds in 254/254 cells, median margin 2.5×, but non-vacuous only on the 89 anchored-persona cells (median λ=0.25); OLS slope 0.88, R²=0.74 for the first-moment prediction (App. 13.3). N_eff on the testbed: Barabási–Albert 3.0→9.8, Erdős–Rényi 2.8→11.1, regular graph 12.0→12.0 (Table 4). Herding is gated by social power: testbed error 5.5 at ζ=0 vs ≈42 for any ζ>0 (Table 3). Cross-family: Llama-3.1-8B narrow 0.02, equalized 0.38, wide ceiling 0.27 (Table 11). Boundary environments (App. 13.8): Debate gap +0.07; GovSim saturates (survival 12 months, sustainability 1.0 in every condition, n=5–24); MARBLE placement effect flips sign across replicates (+0.61 vs −0.58), reported as a negative result.
- **Controls / runs.** Adversary-off control; 2×2 (adversary × allocator) at n=8 (Table 5); 5 seeds per cell (8 and 24 in the testbed; 1000 bootstrap resamples for N_eff); ablations over ζ, λ, Sinkhorn k, five structural variants (memory, exposure-price, late-joiner, distance-decay, skeptic); greedy vs τ=0.7; second model family. Hardware and library versions listed; GPU lockfile not committed (App. 12.6).
- **Stated limitations.** No limitations section in the main text. In the appendix: wide-β "wisdom" is capability-bounded (weak model hurt by wide context, App. 13.7); the two-regime cannot arise where there is nothing to pool (GovSim, Debate); Werewolf equalizer recovery weakens with n; WS sign flip unverified; bridge bound loose off the anchored subset.
- **Weaknesses I see.** (1) The core mechanism is close to tautological: at narrow β the harness deletes the decisive clues from the prompt, so "herding" is "agents cannot pool what they never see" (their words, App. 12.3). (2) The dominant source is a hard-coded, non-updating adversary, not an emergent leader. (3) 7B models, 3–5 rounds, engineered graphs; no structure is formed by the agents. (4) The bridge theorem is only tight when an explicit anchoring prompt is added; the discussion benchmarks run at λ=1 where it says nothing. (5) Its GovSim saturation (all conditions survive) sits oddly next to SovSim's CPR results (o4-mini 40% survival) and the original GovSim finding that few models sustain the resource — different models, so not a contradiction, but a reminder that commons results are fragile to backbone.
- **Nearest prior work they cite.** DeGroot 1974; Friedkin & Johnsen 1990; Golub & Jackson 2010; Bala & Goyal 1998; Acemoglu & Ozdaglar 2011; Banerjee 1992; Bikhchandani et al. 1992; Cho, Guntuku & Ungar 2025 (herd behaviour in LLM MAS); Pitre et al. 2025 CONSENSAGENT; Chuang et al. 2024 opinion dynamics with LLM networks; Papachristou & Yuan 2025 network formation among multi-LLMs (PNAS Nexus); De Marzo et al. 2024 consensus-size constraint; Amayuelas et al. 2024; Taubenfeld et al. 2024; Du et al. 2024; benchmarks HiddenBench (Li, Naito & Shirado 2025), AgentsNet (Grötschla et al. 2025), Werewolf (Xu et al. 2023), GovSim, MultiAgentBench/MARBLE; MoE routing (Shazeer 2017, Fedus 2022, Clark 2022, Wang 2024).

## 5. arXiv 2602.23093 — Mori & Johnson, "Three AI-agents walk into a bar… 'Lord of the Flies' tribalism emerges among smart AI-Agents"

- **Authors / affiliation / venue / date.** Dhwanil M. Mori, Neil F. Johnson; Dynamic Online Networks Laboratory / Physics Department, George Washington University. Dated February 2026; submitted 26 Feb 2026 (cs.AI, cs.SI, physics.soc-ph). Venue not stated. Code "available from the authors on reasonable request".
- **Environment (§3).** El Farol / minority game. N∈{3,4} agents, capacity C∈{1,2}, 30–100 rounds. Each round every agent outputs GO/STAY; payoff +1 if GO and A_t≤C or STAY and A_t>C, else −1 (Eq. 1). Agents see a sliding window of 5–10 rounds of attendance and their own outcomes; **no communication**; not told the others are LLMs; told N and C and to maximise cumulative payoff. Six personality prompts (neutral, risk-averse, contrarian, trend-follower, optimist, pessimist; Table 1). Models: "small" (Gemini 2.5 Flash, GPT-3.5-turbo-0125, Claude 3 Haiku) vs "large" (Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4-turbo-2024-04-09, Claude Sonnet 4.5); ε-greedy (ε=0.15) vs temperature 0.7. Whether model families are mixed within a run: not stated clearly. 43 runs, 154 agent instances (§1.5). **Persistent artifacts: none** (attendance history). **Payoff: explicit.** **Enforcement: none.** Baselines: capacity-matching random p=C/N (analytic overload 25.9% at (3,1), 29.6% at (3,2), 31.25% at (4,2), App. A) and coin-flip p=0.5.
- **What is measured.** System overload frequency/severity, waste, fairness, starvation; per-agent overload contribution rate (Eq. 3); effective diversity S_eff from pairwise action correlations (Eq. 4). "Tribes" = k-means clusters (k=3, silhouette 0.458, Ward hierarchical check) over five per-agent metrics (request frequency, max starvation, burst variance, efficiency, overload contribution) across the 154 instances (§4.5, Table 3): Opportunistic 48.1% (n=74; request freq 0.845; overload contribution 0.737; efficiency 0.131; mean max starvation 35.0), Aggressive 27.3% (n=42; 0.586; 0.206; 0.624; 10.6), Conservative 24.7% (n=38; 0.200; 0.183; 0.101; 73.5 — three agents never served in 100 rounds). No "Steady" (near p=C/N) cluster. Kruskal–Wallis η² 0.19–0.74; χ²(personality, cluster)=40.4, df=10, p<0.001 — risk-averse → Conservative 58%, optimist/trend-follower → Opportunistic 73–74%, neutral → Aggressive 43%, contrarian → Opportunistic 52% (§4.5). Model size (§4.2, N=4, C=2): large 72.5% overload vs small 53.8% vs baseline 31.25% (2.32× and 1.72×). Homogeneous-personality runs (§4.3, N=3, C=1, all large models): risk-averse 25.0% (vs 25.9% target, within noise), contrarian 70.0%, trend-follower 81.0%, optimist 89.0%, coin flip 50.0%; S_eff 2.88–2.98 (Table 2). ε-greedy preserves decision-level stochasticity better than temperature (§4.4).
- **Controls / runs.** Analytic random baselines. 43 runs total across all conditions; no per-condition n, no CIs, no significance tests on the headline model-size comparison (§6.1). Design is explicitly confounded: "The 43 runs vary N, C, model size, personality prompts, and exploration mechanism simultaneously" (§6.1); all homogeneous runs used large models; pessimist and neutral homogeneous runs were not conducted (Table 2 note).
- **Stated limitations (§5.3, §6.1).** N∈{3,4} far from real infrastructure; 100 rounds; fixed C; no communication; prompt-wording sensitivity; confounded model-size comparison; starvation metric not comparable across 30- vs 100-round runs; cluster-separability p-values are descriptive because the same features define and test clusters and agents within a run are coupled; model versions incompletely reported.
- **Weaknesses I see.** (1) "Tribes" and "Lord of the Flies" describe clusters of behavioural statistics of agents that never interact except through an attendance count; there is no group identity, no in-group behaviour, no communication. (2) Cluster membership is 52–74% determined by the personality prompt, so the taxonomy is largely prompt-driven. (3) The headline claim (smarter → dumber) rests on two uncontrolled percentages. (4) Silhouette 0.458 on the clustering features themselves.
- **Nearest prior work they cite (§2).** Arthur's El Farol; Challet & Zhang minority game; crowd–anticrowd theory; Chmura & Pitz human El Farol; Whitehead RL sorting; Chen & Gostoli; Akata et al. (LLMs in repeated 2×2 games); Guo et al.; Fan et al. negotiation; FAIRGAME and Huynh et al.; LLM-Coordination benchmark; Abdelnabi et al.; ECON; **Takata et al.** (LLM agents in a spatial El Farol with 20 agents who communicate and form groups — the closest neighbour, §2.3); Konstantakopoulos et al. building energy; Jo et al. fairness incompatibilities.

## 6. Named near-neighbours (abstract + setup only)

### 6a. Ashery, Aiello & Baronchelli, "Emergent social conventions and collective bias in LLM populations", Science Advances 11, eadu9368 (2025); arXiv 2410.08948
- **Setup (Experimental Setup; Materials and Methods).** Naming game. Population N=24 (robustness to N=200), name pool W=10 letters (up to 26), memory H=5 past interactions. Each step two random agents are paired; each outputs a name; match → +100 to both, mismatch → −50 to both. Prompt gives rules, the H-interaction memory (partner's choice, own choice, success, cumulative score), and asks the agent to maximise its own tally "conditional on the behavior of their co-player". **No incentive for global consensus; agents are not told they are in a population.** Names presented in randomised order. Homogeneous populations of Llama-2-70b-Chat (4-bit), Llama-3-70B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, Claude-3.5-Sonnet; non-zero temperature with top-K sampling; meta-prompting comprehension check. 40 runs per model (27 and 20 for Claude and Llama-2 in Fig. 2A). Committed-minority experiments: adversarial agents always output the alternative name; flip = 95% success over the past 3N interactions.
- **Findings.** Global convention by population round ~15 for all models but Llama-2; symmetry breaking (winner-take-all); collective bias toward a "strong" name that is absent at interaction 1 and appears by interaction 3 through asymmetric memory-state strategies (Table 1; p(M|{1:M,Q;2:Q,M})=0.848 vs p(Q|{1:Q,M;2:M,Q})=0.451); with the full alphabet the population converges on 'A' because individuals prefer it; critical mass to flip a consensus ranges from 2% (Llama-3) to 67% (Llama-2), and Llama-3.1 flips spontaneously from the weak to the strong convention.
- **Relevance to the draft.** The cleanest existing demonstration that a population-level convention arises with no global goal, and the cleanest demonstration that *cross-run convergence on the same choice can be a model prior, not a functional necessity*. Any claim of "independent reinvention" across replicates has to rule out this collective-bias mechanism.

### 6b. Altera (Ahn et al.), "Project Sid: Many-agent simulations toward AI civilization", arXiv 2411.00114 (31 Oct 2024; technical report, not peer-reviewed)
- **Setup.** PIANO architecture (concurrent modules + cognitive-controller bottleneck) on GPT-4o (older base models "not possible", App. A). Minecraft. Single-agent progression: 25 agents, 30 min, 5 repeats; 49 agents, 4 h. Societies: 50 agents, 4 h (12 in-game days). Specialisation (§5.1, Methods 8.2): 30 identical-personality agents, same community goal ("survive… and create an efficient Minecraft Village"), 20 minutes, 3 simulations per village type (normal/martial/art); roles inferred by a GPT-4o call from rolling windows of 5 self-generated social goals; ablation removes the social-awareness module. Collective rules (§5.2, Methods 8.3): 29 agents (25 constituents, 3 pro- or anti-tax influencers, 1 remote election-manager agent), 20-minute runs, 20% tax constitution, feedback → election manager writes amendments → vote → new constitution at the 10-minute mark; 5 tax seasons before and after; 4 repeats per configuration; conditions: full architecture, frozen constitution, module ablation. "We chose not to incorporate guards or police." Cultural transmission (§5.3): **one** 500-agent run, 9000 s, 6 towns, 20 Pastafarian priests; memes extracted by LM summarisation; religion tracked by keyword occurrence; 1000+-agent runs exceeded the server's capacity.
- **Findings.** Roles (farmer, miner, engineer, guard, explorer, blacksmith…) persist per agent and vanish without the social module (entropy, Fig. 8E); role-specific actions (Fig. 9). Constituents pay ~20% of inventory under the initial constitution; after an influencer-driven amendment to 5–10% they pay 9%; frozen constitution → no change; ablation → no bidirectional change (Fig. 10). Memes require town density; Pastafarian converts increase without saturating over 2 h.
- **Relevance to the draft.** The only prior work with (i) a persistent shared world, (ii) a written rule that agents obey without any enforcement (guards were deliberately omitted), and (iii) rule amendment. But the rule, the amendment machinery and the election manager are designer-built; horizon is 20 minutes; and the authors themselves state the agents "cannot simulate de novo emergence of societal innovations and infrastructures, such as the emergence of democratic systems, fiat economies, or communication systems" (§7).

### (b) What this line establishes

1. **Conventions and consensus form fast from local interaction, and what they converge on is partly a model prior.** Ashery et al. show universal conventions by ~15 population rounds with no global incentive, and that the *identity* of the winning convention is biased by the model even when individuals are unbiased. SNLA shows that whether consensus is informative or herded is decided by how much of the population each agent actually reads, not by the visible graph.
2. **Population-scale structure is heavy-tailed activity stratification.** MoltBook's only robust structural finding is a core–periphery split (93.5% periphery, 0.08% hubs) with power-law cascades; its "roles" are activity bins. This matches human platforms and says little about functional differentiation.
3. **Imposed asymmetry is corrosive, and social stakes shape speech.** SovSim: a single uncapped last-mover collapses commons that symmetric agents of the same model sustain; lying by the sovereign redistributes but does not further shorten survival. LLMAgora: relational stakes with no stated objective move ~40% of public decisions away from the private channel, with strong model heterogeneity (Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-OSS-120B ≈10%, Gemini 3.1 Pro ≈90%).
4. **In resource games LLM agents do not self-organise to calibrated policies.** Tribalism: no agent cluster approximates p=C/N; the clusters track the personality prompt.
5. **Given designer-built institutions, agents comply and amend them without enforcement.** Project Sid: 20% tax paid with no police; amendment changes payment; frozen constitution changes nothing.
6. **Methodological regularities.** (i) "Emergent structure" is nearly always a clustering of output statistics or a treatment imposed by prompt/graph; the genuinely emergent objects (Ashery's conventions; Sid's role choice; MoltBook's Claw Republic) are either trivial (a letter), designer-scaffolded, or unmeasured. (ii) Homogeneous-backbone populations dominate; only MoltBook (unrecorded) and SNLA's 7B/1.5B society are heterogeneous. (iii) Replication counts are 5 seeds or fewer per cell everywhere except Ashery (40) and MoltBook (0). (iv) Horizons are 3–100 rounds or ≤4 hours; the one multi-week dataset is observational. (v) Nobody replicates anyone else; the one overlapping environment (GovSim in SovSim vs SNLA) gives incompatible pictures on different backbones. (vi) The good control to copy is SovSim App. A.6 (neutral labels rule out the literary-trope explanation) and Ashery's individual-vs-collective bias test.

### (c) What none of them do — the gap, concretely

The draft's setting is, as I understand it: many agents working over a long horizon in a **real shared filesystem**, with **no task and no reward**, an **enforcement regime that is announced but not actually running**, **institutions** (preregistration, hash anchoring, machine-checkable certificates) that the agents build themselves, and **independent reinvention of the same institutions on isolated replicate machines**. Against the seven works above:

| Dimension | Closest existing point | Distance |
|---|---|---|
| Real shared filesystem / production of durable artifacts | MoltBook (persistent feed; 317 code-module cascades tracked by content hash) and Sid (Minecraft world, community chests). Everything else is a chat transcript or a scalar. | No paper has agents reading and writing files that other agents build on; no paper's "artifact" is checkable (MoltBook's Q score parses code with `ast` but on posted snippets, not a repository). |
| No task, no reward | Ashery (local payoff only, no global goal); MoltBook (no task, karma only); LLMAgora (no stated objective but scripted stakes); Sid (community-goal prompt). SovSim, Tribalism, SNLA all have explicit payoffs or a correct answer. | Nobody runs a population with neither a scoring criterion nor a prompt-supplied goal and then measures what they build. |
| Long horizon | MoltBook 3 weeks (observational, humans in the loop); Sid 4 h / 2.5 h; SovSim and GovSim 12 rounds; SNLA 3–5 rounds; Tribalism ≤100 rounds. | MoltBook's authors say 3 weeks is "insufficient to capture … institutional consolidation or stable norm evolution" (v2 §7.3). No controlled experiment exceeds a few hours. |
| Enforcement announced but not running | Sid is the only precedent for a rule obeyed with no enforcer (guards deliberately omitted; 20% tax paid). SovSim's boss cap is a hard constraint, not an announcement. Nobody manipulates the *belief* that enforcement exists against its *presence*. | The announced-vs-running distinction is untested. Sid shows compliance under a designer-authored rule for 20 minutes; it cannot say whether compliance persists when the promised sanction never arrives. |
| Institutions emerging (preregistration, hash anchoring, certificates) | Sid: constitution and election machinery designer-built; authors state de novo institutional emergence is out of reach (§7). MoltBook: "written manifesto and draft constitution" reported as an anecdote in v1 §1, unmeasured, dropped in v2. SovSim: the cap is called an Ostrom institution but is imposed. | No paper observes agents inventing a verification or commitment device. No paper has an institution that is machine-checkable (a hash, a certificate, a preregistered prediction) rather than a natural-language rule. |
| Independent reinvention across isolated replicates | Ashery: 40 runs converge on the same "strong" convention — but that is the collective-bias result, i.e. convergence explained by model prior, not by function, and different runs otherwise break symmetry differently. Sid: 3–5 repeats summarised by role-distribution entropy, not by whether the same specific solutions recur. SovSim/SNLA/LLMAgora: 5 seeds, reported as means. | Cross-replicate identity of *specific invented mechanisms* is not measured anywhere. The nearest analogue (Ashery) is also the strongest alternative explanation the draft must exclude: same-backbone replicates may "reinvent" the same institution because the base model prefers it, exactly as Llama populations prefer the letter 'A'. A neutral-label / alternative-backbone control in the spirit of SovSim App. A.6 and a first-interaction individual-bias test in the spirit of Ashery Table 1 are the two controls the literature would expect. |
| Heterogeneous populations | MoltBook (unrecorded mix); SNLA (7B hubs + 1.5B); Tribalism (small vs large, mixing unclear). | If the draft's replicates share one backbone, it is in the majority; if they mix, it is nearly alone, and model heterogeneity becomes a confound for reinvention claims. |
| Measurement of institutions rather than of activity | Sid (tax paid vs rule; amendment adopted); LLMAgora (public vs private channel); SovSim (leader extraction). | The field has no operationalisation of "an institution emerged": nobody defines the observable, its onset time, or its cross-run recurrence rate. This is the open methodological slot. |

**Bottom line for positioning.** The five papers establish that LLM populations stratify, converge, herd, comply, and cave to stakes — all in settings where the structure is either put in by hand or is a statistic on outputs, with horizons of minutes to hours, homogeneous backbones, and ≤5 seeds. The two named neighbours establish the two boundary cases: conventions bootstrap from nothing (Ashery) and designer-built rules are obeyed without police (Sid). Nothing in this batch has (i) agent-built, machine-checkable institutions, (ii) an announced-but-absent enforcer, (iii) a multi-day open-ended production setting with no task, or (iv) a cross-replicate reinvention measurement. The strongest objection the batch supplies against a reinvention claim is Ashery's collective bias; the strongest design borrowed from the batch to answer it is SovSim's label ablation plus Ashery's isolated-agent bias test.
